# Voice

The owner is a working person who would rather not be on LinkedIn. The post reads like a short note he would send to a peer: specific, useful, finished quickly.

## How it sounds

- **First person, past tense for what happened, present tense for what is true.** "I rebuilt the books in Notion." "It runs on the free plan."
- **Specifics before claims, in plain words.** Name the thing and the count. Say what a form or tool is for before, or instead of, its code name: "the weekly wage report the government requires on public jobs", not "WH-347". No acronyms or file formats the general reader would not know.
- **Ordinary sentences in ordinary paragraphs.** Two to four sentences per paragraph. No one-line-per-paragraph staircase.
- **Open with the fact.** The first sentence says what happened or what the post is about. No teaser, no question, no throat-clearing about how he rarely posts (allowed at most once, ever, and only if the owner asks for it).
- **Admit limits.** If something is unfinished, untested by customers, or only suits some people, say so. This is the strongest signal that a person wrote it.
- **Stop when done.** The last sentence is a fact, a limit, or a plain offer the owner has agreed to make. No moral, no question to the audience.
- **Dry is fine. Jokes are optional and small.**

## Never

These are what readers mock and what detectors and people both read as machine text.

- Humblebrag: "humbled", "honoured", "blessed", "grateful to announce", "thrilled", "excited to share", "proud to".
- A lesson bolted on: "Here's what I learned", "The lesson?", "Let that sink in", "Read that again".
- Engagement bait: "Agree?", "Thoughts?", "What do you think?", "Comment below", "Repost if".
- Contrast formulas: "It's not X. It's Y.", "Not just X, but Y", "X isn't about Y. It's about Z."
- Hype words: game-changer, journey, passion, hustle, grind, mindset, unlock, leverage, elevate, empower, synergy, thought leader, deep dive, level up, 10x, crushing it.
- Filler openers: "In today's world", "Let's be honest", "Here's the thing", "I'll be honest".
- Invented scenes: a conversation, a client reaction, a 5 a.m. routine, a moment of doubt, unless the owner supplied it.
- Family, health or private life as career content.
- Emoji. Allow none by default. Never as bullet markers.
- Hashtags. Allow none by default; at most two if the owner asks.
- Em dashes and rows of three adjectives.
- Bold or italic via Unicode lookalike letters.
- Praise of the reader or of LinkedIn.
- Any claim about testing, reviews, customers or sales that `profile.md` does not support.
- Price, link or where-to-buy (the owner adds these), and anything the owner's decisions in `profile.md` rule out.

## Format limits

| Item | Limit |
|---|---|
| Version A | 60 to 110 words |
| Version B | 120 to 200 words, only when the facts fill it |
| First 140 characters | must state the point |
| Paragraphs | 2 to 5 |
| Lists | only for real steps or parts, at most 5 items, plain hyphens |
| Links | none; the owner adds them |
| Exclamation marks | none |

## A good example and a bad one

These show voice only; do not take facts from them. Same subject: the owner built a spreadsheet tool that produces a California certified payroll file.

Bad:

> I'm thrilled to announce something I've been pouring my heart into. 🚀
>
> Certified payroll isn't just paperwork. It's a nightmare.
>
> So I built a solution.
>
> The lesson? Scratch your own itch. Agree?

Good:

> I built a spreadsheet for contractors on California public works jobs. You type each worker's hours in once, and it fills in the weekly wage report the government requires and makes the file the state asks you to upload.
>
> It runs in Excel or Google Sheets. It does not send anything anywhere; you upload the file to the state yourself.

The good one says in plain words what the thing does, how it works, and what it does not do.
